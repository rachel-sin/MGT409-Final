Celiac-Safe DC Restaurant Ranker

Evidence-based gluten-free safety — not just “has a GF menu”

A web app: search a DC restaurant by name, or browse by neighborhood and cuisine — get back a ranked, evidence-backed safety verdict, with a source behind every claim.

Rachel Sin · MGT409 Final Project

Background & the problem
Who needs this — and why today's workarounds don't actually solve it.
The problem

Celiac disease is an autoimmune condition — even trace gluten exposure can cause real illness, sometimes for days. There's no “probably fine” margin for error, but also no reliable way to know in advance which kitchen is actually safe.

How people cope today
  • Word of mouth / asking the restaurant directlyAccurate in the moment, but doesn't scale, and depends on staff actually knowing their own kitchen.
  • Google / YelpHuge coverage, but reviews are written for food quality, not celiac safety — a glowing review can still hide an unsafe kitchen.
  • GF-specific directories (Find Me Gluten Free)Built for this exact need, but incomplete coverage, and the verdict is still a crowd-sourced star average, not a verified safety tier.
Every current workaround gives you an average opinion — not a verified answer.
What is new here
Find Me Gluten Free shows Ama at 4.5★ — buried on that page:
  • 5-tier evidence taxonomy, not a star averageOne honest outcome per restaurant, from “confirmed dedicated gluten-free prep” down to “credible illness report” — never a blended score hiding the bad among the good.
  • Asymmetric-risk rule enforced in code, not just a promptA credible illness report always overrides positive reviews, re-checked in code after the AI responds.
  • Verbatim quote + source link behind every claimEvery claim is a word-for-word quote, independently re-verified — never a paraphrase.
If any reviewer got sick, this app tells you right away instead of burying it in an average.
The agents
5 PydanticAI agents, each with one narrow job, on gpt-6-luna.
What each agent actually does
  1. Finder (confirm) — verifies one named restaurant is real and finds its address
  2. Finder (discover) — finds a list of candidates for a neighborhood/cuisine browse
  3. Evidence Extractor — pulls real quotes from the restaurant's own site, then third-party reviews
  4. Skeptic — flags marketing language and claims that contradict each other
  5. Ranker — weighs everything into one final safety tier
Two code-level checks re-verify the riskiest rules after the Ranker responds, rather than trusting the model's word alone.

Impact
Measured quality gains and real users' feedback shipped directly.
  • Accuracy against independent human judgment: 90% → 100% after a real bug fix A 10-restaurant test set, hand-labeled by reading the raw evidence directly (not copied from the system's own output), started at 90% agreement. Real user feedback during testing separately exposed a Skeptic bug — fixing it corrected the one restaurant that had been mismatched, and a full re-evaluation then hit 10/10.
  • Three testers' exact words shipped three real fixes Tester #1's confusion over the search form led to a dedicated restaurant-name field and clearer homepage copy. Tester #2's question about the 3-result limit shipped as “Show more restaurants,” which in testing also surfaced two further bugs (a stale count, an unclear sort order). Tester #3 — a family member who actually has celiac disease — called the app helpful and easy to understand.
  • 3 of 16 reviews (19%) on Ama's page are illness reports — invisible in its headline rating Our system surfaces the worst of those three as the single top-line verdict, with zero additional reading required.
Listening to real users already made this tool more accurate and easier to use.
Limits + next steps
Honest gaps, not hidden ones.
  • Thin real-user sample — three testers so far
