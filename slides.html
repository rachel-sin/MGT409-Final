Celiac-Safe DC Restaurant Ranker

Evidence-based gluten-free safety — not just “has a GF menu”

Rachel Sin · MGT409 Final Project

The problem
The same page that shows a reassuring 4.5★ also hides this.
A star rating can hide the one bad report that matters most to someone with celiac disease.
What is new here
A wrong restaurant choice isn’t a bad meal — it’s a real illness.
  • 5-tier evidence taxonomy, not a star average
  • Asymmetric-risk rule enforced in code, not just a prompt
  • Verbatim quote + source link behind every claim
If any reviewer got sick, this app tells you right away instead of burying it in an average.
The agents
5 PydanticAI agents, each with one narrow job, on gpt-6-luna.
A computer program double-checks every AI recommendation before it reaches you.
A real traced run
A live search for Adams Morgan, sorted best-evidence-first.
Once restaurants are found, they're always listed strongest-evidence-first, never in a random order.

Safety: a defense working, live
Ama has real positive accommodation evidence — and a credible illness report.
Even with good reviews, one credible report of someone getting sick always wins and shows as a warning.
Impact
Measured quality gains and a real user's feedback shipped directly.
  • Accuracy against an independent expert's judgment: 90% → 100% after a real bug fix
  • A real tester's exact words shipped two real fixes
  • 3 of 16 reviews (19%) on Ama's page are illness reports — invisible in its headline rating
Listening to one real user already made this tool more accurate and easier to use.
Limits + next steps
Honest gaps, not hidden ones.
  • Thin real-user sample — one full tester so far
  • User-submitted finds not yet supported
  • Cross-neighborhood redirect suggested, not yet built
  • Single city (DC) today
This works today, but it still needs many more real people to try it before it's truly reliable.
